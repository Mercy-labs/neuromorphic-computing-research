Neuromorphic Computing Research

Research area: Computation Beyond Conventional Neural Networks · Neuromorphic Computing · Alternative Architectures

Overview

This repository documents an ongoing investigation into alternative computational architectures, with a focus on neuromorphic computing, event-driven computation, and the relationship between memory and processing.

The goal is to understand how these architectures work, evaluate their limitations, and investigate whether alternative computational approaches can improve efficiency on specific tasks.

This is an exploratory research project. Proposed improvements will be treated as hypotheses and tested through experiments rather than assumed.

Research Question

Can event-driven computational architectures reduce unnecessary computation and data movement while maintaining comparable task performance to conventional computational approaches?

Research Objectives

- Study the principles and limitations of conventional computational architectures.
- Investigate event-driven computation and neuromorphic architectures.
- Explore sparse activity, local interactions, adaptive state changes, and memory–computation integration.
- Develop software simulations and reproducible experiments.
- Compare alternative architectures using measurable performance criteria.
- Document findings, limitations, and future research directions.

Research Methodology

1. Literature review: Study existing research and identify open questions.
2. Architecture design: Define the computational models to investigate.
3. Implementation: Build experimental prototypes using appropriate software tools.
4. Benchmarking: Compare models under consistent experimental conditions.
5. Analysis: Evaluate task performance, computational activity, latency, memory traffic, and energy where reliable measurements are available.
6. Documentation: Publish methods, results, code, and limitations.

Research Principles

- Reproducibility over unsupported claims.
- Evidence over assumptions.
- Fair comparisons with established baselines.
- Transparent reporting of unsuccessful experiments and limitations.
- Proper acknowledgement of existing research.

Current Status

Phase 1: Research foundations and literature review.

The initial focus is understanding existing architectures, defining a testable hypothesis, and establishing a suitable experimental methodology.

No performance advantage is claimed at this stage.
